# Premium Shorts research and blueprint v2

Prepared 18 November 2026. Nine YouTube Shorts across four channels were verified
above one million public views. Counts ranged from 1,088,485 to 5,509,033 at
retrieval. The visual audit used 72 timed browser-rendered stills and the
available English automatic captions. No source video/audio files were downloaded.

The strongest change for our next film is to make the images demonstrate the
mechanism. The current rage-bait renderer relies heavily on centered headlines,
floating cutouts, small settling motion and paper wipes. The new skill calls
for a coherent story world, persistent anchors, causal object interactions,
mechanism reveals and motivated changes of camera scale.

## What this study establishes

The view totals and clip identities were verified through public YouTube player
metadata. Eight sampled frames per video support the specific composition and
object-continuity observations below. English captions were automatic and can
contain mistakes; the bookshelf clip's available captions start at 6.5 seconds.
The Suez player showed localized captions, so English ASR was fetched separately.

This is a purposive example study, not an exhaustive ranking, a controlled
experiment, or proof that an editing technique caused the view count. Exact
keyframe curves, production applications and source sound design were not
observed. No private retention, swipe, revenue or engaged-view data were available.

YouTube's official guide says that, since 31 March 2025, Shorts views count
starts and replays without a minimum watch-time requirement. Engaged views
remain a separate Analytics metric. Public million-view totals therefore do
not establish strong retention by themselves.
Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en

## Changes that matter most

1. **Construct a world.** Stage foreground, subject and background with a shared
   material system, light direction and spatial logic. A paper desk, post,
   comment and hidden control can belong to the same scene.
2. **Demonstrate the hidden mechanism.** Turn, open, cross-section or follow
   something so the viewer sees cause and consequence. Do not rely on an arrow
   and a big headline to perform the whole explanation.
3. **Carry objects across changes.** Register the same anchor through a match
   cut or camera change. The map, rock, cup or post remains recognizable while
   its context, scale or role changes.
4. **Author the movement.** Plan acceleration, travel, deceleration and a hold.
   Add restrained depth/parallax, alpha-matte reveals and transform motion blur.
   One camera intention per shot; avoid a perpetual zoom or random wobble.
5. **Separate timing systems.** Keep handmade paper poses at 12 fps, with camera,
   world wrappers, mattes, type and captions at 30 fps where appropriate.
6. **Reduce competing text.** A diagram/action can carry a beat without a new
   headline. Use attached labels and a stable, readable caption band inside
   the user-supplied safe zones.
7. **Plan events rather than constant hard cuts.** Meaningful new information
   still arrives every 1.2–2.0s, but several events can happen in one coherent
   continuous scene.
8. **Use material-specific sound.** Paper movement, a keypress or an impact
   deserves a cue. Constant whooshes are not an editing plan. This sound advice
   is our production recommendation, not a finding from an audio audit.

## What we should not copy

- Several references end in a subscribe/CTA frame. Our requested grammatical
  and visual loop remains binding.
- Some source captions, labels and chrome fall outside our custom mobile safe
  zones. Their placement is not permission to violate those zones.
- The 51–155s source runtimes do not replace the user's 28–45s target.
- Zack D. Films is an adjacent 3D mechanism reference. Its shot logic can be
  adapted to paper/2.5D without adopting a full character-animation pipeline.
- The source videos' scientific or economic claims are not independent factual
  research for our scripts. Keep a fresh primary-source claims ledger.

## Concrete effect on the next rage-bait edit

Keep the validated narration structure and loop, then redesign the visuals:
start inside one post/phone world; follow a correction into replies; uncover
the attention incentive behind that thread; show why a reaction is not a
guaranteed boost; offer an exit; return to the registered opening post/cursor.

The supplied example plan has six continuous story scenes with 23 meaningful
visual events. It is an authoring plan, not a newly rendered rage-bait video.
The two already-delivered films and their original project ZIPs were preserved.
