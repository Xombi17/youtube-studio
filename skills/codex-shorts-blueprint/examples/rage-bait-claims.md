# Verified claim ledger — Rage bait

Verified 18 November 2026, before the production script was finalized.

## C1 — Deliberate provocation for engagement

Source: Oxford University Press, “The Oxford Word of the Year 2025 is rage bait,”
published 1 December 2025.
https://corp.oup.com/news/the-oxford-word-of-the-year-2025-is-rage-bait/

Verified passage: “online content deliberately designed to elicit anger or outrage
by being frustrating, provocative, or offensive, typically posted in order to
increase traffic to or engagement with a particular web page or social media content”.

Permitted script claim: rage bait deliberately provokes outrage to attract
engagement. The argument *might* be the intended result of a fictional post.
Do not infer that every bad video is intentional or that a specific real creator
is using this tactic. No real creator is depicted.

## C2 — Recommendations use multiple signals, including satisfaction

Source: YouTube Help, “How YouTube recommendations work.”
https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387?hl=en

Verified passages: “Our recommendation system is constantly evolving, learning
every day from over 80 billion pieces of information we call signals.”
“User surveys that ask you to rate videos that you watched helps the system
understand satisfaction, not just watch time.”

The document separately lists watch history, search history, subscriptions,
likes, dislikes, Not interested, and Don't recommend channel.

Permitted script claim: YouTube also considers feedback and satisfaction.
“A comment isn't a magic boost” is a caution against claiming a guaranteed
algorithmic outcome; it is not a claim about a published numeric comment weight.
Do not claim all engagement is positive or that comments guarantee reach,
revenue, virality, or a particular recommendation outcome.

## C3 — Not interested is a real feedback option

Same official YouTube Help source. Verified passage:
“Videos you mark as ‘Not interested’ helps to inform what to avoid recommending
in the future.”

Permitted script suggestion: swipe away or use Not interested. The film uses
an illustrative paper menu, not a precise tutorial to a particular app version.

## C4 — Staged narrative and editorial questions

The absurd food hack, comments, correction, argument, symbolic counters and
puppetry are fictional illustrations of C1. They are not screenshots, real
engagement data, real food advice, or allegations about identifiable people.
“Ever corrected an obviously terrible take?” is a viewer reflection, not a
statistical claim. Attention is the editorial through-line; no diagnosis or
claim of brain damage is made.

All URLs and AI-production disclosures belong in metadata/post copy. None are
burned onto the canvas, following the user's supplied blueprint.
